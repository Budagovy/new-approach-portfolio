SIMPLY PIANO / WEEKLY CHALLENGE

A weekend worth
coming back for.

I designed a recurring music challenge that gave children a reason to return at the weekend. Working in a POD, I shaped the challenge journey, progress and rewards, and built the Figma template for new weekly themes.

MY ROLE
Senior Product Designer in a POD team
WORKING WITH
Product, development and music specialists
STATUS
Released to all users

Three songs, a weekend deadline and visible progress toward a music reward.

THE PROBLEM & THE SOLUTION

Give practice a reason to happen again.

THE PROBLEM
Children stopped practicing. Parents cancelled subscriptions.
We were seeing children disengage after Course 2. We wanted to give them something to look forward to and a reason to return for another practice session.

THE SOLUTION
A weekly challenge built around music they love.
Children play three songs within a familiar theme, earn a song package, and can keep improving toward a certificate. The event returns each week with new content.

OUR HYPOTHESIS

A weekly event with familiar music, clear progress and relevant rewards could bring children back to practice and help reduce subscription cancellations.

KEY DECISION / REWARDS

Make the music worth earning.

The reward we could offer was more music. I built the experience around that constraint: three challenging songs lead to a package of easier songs in a theme the child already enjoys. The reward gives them something new to play after the challenge.

FIRST GOAL
A song package.
Earn at least two stars in each of the three songs. The music-note icon turns gold when this goal is reached.

NEXT GOAL
A certificate of achievement.
After the song package is earned, the progress bar expands to reveal the final goal, keeping the earned music-note icon visible. The certificate is awarded only when the child completes this additional stage, earning three stars in each song — nine in total.

The certificate is sent to the parent’s email. I wanted it to give the child something to proudly show their parent: a way to celebrate their success together and bring the parent into the learning experience.

Keep the path forward visible.

The music specialists and pedagogical advisers set the difficulty and completion thresholds. My job was to make those rules clear in the interface. Children can retry until the event ends; only their best result for each song counts, so progress reflects improvement.

First milestone: at least two stars in every song unlocks the music package.

Final milestone: three stars in every song unlocks the certificate, emailed to parents.

KEY DECISION / THE WEEKLY EXPERIENCE

Make the challenge feel like an event.

During prototype testing, we noticed that children focused on the challenge’s theme and story while overlooking other information when the screen became too busy.

I made the theme the entry point and gave the lobby a clear hierarchy: three songs at the center, stars on each card and progress toward the current reward above. The next milestone appears after the first reward is earned. The aim was to turn excitement about the music into a clear next action, without asking children to take in the entire challenge at once.

A countdown introduces the event before it starts. When it ends, the experience recognizes the child’s achievement and introduces the next challenge. This connects one weekend to the next.

Before the event: the theme and countdown give children something specific to return for.

After the final goal: a themed certificate makes the achievement shareable with the family.

A consistent structure across different themes.

The BTS and KPop Demon Hunters examples use the same challenge structure. Music and content specialists selected relevant themes and adapted the arrangements for each segment; I designed how the event, progress and rewards were presented.

DESIGN SYSTEM / CONTENT PRODUCTION

One template, powered by AI.
A new theme each week.

A weekly feature also needs a repeatable way to make the next event. I created a Figma template with defined places for the challenge name, palette, artwork, song covers and icons. The team could change the theme while keeping the experience consistent.

Palette and contrast references from the working Figma template.

Artwork placement and preview rules keep new themes within the same structure.

The template specifies contrast checks for text, buttons and the in-game background. It also defines artwork placement and which elements stay fixed, including the gold treatment of earned badges. These rules give each theme room to change while keeping the interface recognizable.

Where AI fits into the workflow.

Our team built an AI-assisted workflow that reviews social trends, creates a moodboard and generates theme assets, then feeds them into the Figma template. My contribution was the template and its design rules, which give the generated assets a consistent place in the product.

VALIDATION & OUTCOME

More sessions.
Fewer cancellations.

We developed the concept within the POD, built an initial prototype and tested it with users before running a controlled test. Over approximately three months, the group with Weekly Challenge had more sessions and fewer subscription cancellations than the control group.

ENGAGEMENT

BUSINESS OUTCOME
Fewer subscription cancellations
The feature group had fewer cancellations than the control group.

Weekly Challenge was subsequently released to all users. Exact uplift figures are not available for this case study, so the results are reported directionally.

WHAT I LEARNED

Design the event and the way it repeats.

The challenge needed both a clear path for the child and a repeatable process for the team. Familiar music gave children a reason to join; visible progress and two distinct rewards gave them something to work toward. The template supported the next week’s experience without starting the design again.
